## Worked example(s) / formulas
1. SES interval (tied to row 6, arithmetic mine): alpha = 0.3, residual sigma = 10 units, horizon h = 3. sigma_3 = 10 * sqrt(1 + 0.3^2 * (3-1)) = 10 * sqrt(1.18) = 10.86. 80% interval = forecast +/- 1.28 * 10.86 = forecast +/- 13.9 units. At h = 1 the half-width is 1.28 * 10 = 12.8. So with a small alpha the SES interval widens slowly with horizon (only alpha^2 per extra step); the interval is symmetric around the forecast.
2. Lower bound at zero (my inference, not from a source): a normal-based interval can go below zero for low-volume items; FPP3 says bootstrapped intervals can be asymmetric (row 5), which is the documented alternative to simply clipping at zero.
3. Fixed percentage vs error-based (rows 13-14, 1): the fixed +/-p% band is the same relative width for every item and horizon; the error-based band is c * sigma_h, which differs per item (noisy items wider) and grows with horizon (rows 3, 4, 6).

## Leads worth chasing (named entities, contradictions, opened questions)
- Hyndman & Koehler (2006) "Another look at measures of forecast accuracy" (MASE, sMAPE critique): primary source for accuracy pitfalls, not fetched.
- Goodwin, Onkal & Thomson (2010, EJOR 205:195-201): fetch the full paper or a citing review for the "85% interval heuristic" details; relevant to whether to show a range or a single recommended number.
- Niu & Harvey (2022) on lay judgment from intervals vs points; Spiegelhalter / Hullman-style work on visualising uncertainty and "deterministic construal error" (misreading ranges as a single value).
- WAPE/WMAPE and bias definitions: need a primary or textbook source (e.g. Hyndman blog, Kolassa's "What are the shortcomings of the MAPE", Vandeput "Data Science for Supply Chain Forecast" on bias/MAE%). Sign-convention conflict between sources must be settled by choosing and stating one.
- Vendor confidence-label documentation (sku.io, Prediko, Toolio, SAP): fetch to confirm tiers and thresholds; note all are vendor material.
- Moving-average prediction intervals: no direct textbook formula found; a typical fallback is to treat the moving average as a forecast with sigma estimated from one-step residuals (not evidenced here).
- S&OP-textbook treatment of scenarios (e.g. Oliver Wight, APICS/ASCM) not retrieved.
